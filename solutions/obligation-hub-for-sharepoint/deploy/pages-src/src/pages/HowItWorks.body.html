Every promise in a signed contract, kept on time

Obligation Hub reads your signed contracts, turns each duty into a tracked item with an owner and a date, and makes sure someone hears about it before it’s due.

What is an obligation?

A duty that starts after a contract is signed: who owes what, by when, and what triggers it. Some fall on a date, some repeat, some wait for an event — and some run for as long as the contract does.

Governing law, headings, definitions and other boilerplate aren’t obligations, so they aren’t tracked. A right the other side has (an audit, say) can still create a duty for us — to cooperate — and that is tracked.

The loop

Four steps, mostly automatic. People step in where judgement matters.

  1. 1

    Add a contract

    Upload the signed file to the Contracts library. Key terms — counterparty, dates, auto-renewal, notice period, value — fill in on their own.

  2. 2

    Captured and classified

    Two capture workflows run the same skill — Capture new contracts when a file is added, Capture changed contracts when one changes. They find each duty, record the clause it came from and classify it. Anything uncertain is held for review.

  3. 3

    Assigned and tracked

    Assign owners gives each obligation an owner and an escalation owner from the routing rules. Timing is tracked from its due date or trigger.

  4. 4

    Reminded, escalated, evidenced

    The Daily sweep reminds owners as dates approach and escalates anything overdue. Owners mark items fulfilled and keep the proof in Evidence.

Who does what

Obligation owner

  • Does the work before the due date
  • Marks the item fulfilled and attaches evidence
  • Starts from My obligations

Contract manager

  • Adds contracts and amendments
  • Clears the Review queue
  • Keeps routing rules, types and exclusions current in Settings

Leader

  • Reads the Friday leadership digest
  • Makes the renewal and exit calls in time
  • Resolves escalations; sees exposure on Leadership

What this sample doesn’t do yet

Known gaps in this sample as it stands today. Plan around them.

  • Events aren’t detected. In the sample, 28 of the 58 open obligations (48%) are triggered by an event or ongoing, so they have no date. Nothing notices the event itself — a change of control, a breach, a termination — a person has to.
  • No proof standard. Mark fulfilled doesn’t require evidence and nobody signs off a fulfilment. The Run Log is an ordinary list people can edit, not an audit trail.
  • Capture is tested on clean samples only. Accuracy was measured on the 11 synthetic Word contracts the skill was tuned on (55 of 55 obligations found). Scans, exhibits, statements of work and documents a contract refers to but that aren’t in the library are untested.
  • Deadlines, not performance. It tracks when things are due. It doesn’t check payments against accounts payable, measure service levels, or connect to e-signature, contract-management or ERP systems.
  • Leadership is a snapshot. The Leadership page shows today only: no trends over time and no dollar exposure.
  • My obligations can’t tell who you are. The page opens the first owner, or the name in its link (#owner=). The My obligations view in the Obligations list does filter to you.
  • Workflows are still in preview. SharePoint AI workflows aren’t generally available yet, messages come from whoever set them up, and runs can fail. Check the Run Log after each run.
  • No per-contract permissions. Everyone with access to the site sees every contract and every obligation.
  • Large lists get slow. Pages load whole lists. Above about 5,000 obligations, index Status, DueDate and Owner, and expect slower pages.
  • Key terms and amendments need a person. Key-term autofill may not run, so fill the columns in by hand if they stay empty. An amendment doesn’t rewrite the original contract’s summary; Contract 360 notes the amendment under it instead.

These are the roadmap, not hidden limits.

Questions

Why was this item held for review?

Capture holds an item when it isn’t confident: it can’t tell who owes the duty, the clause reads more like a right than a duty, it had to calculate a date the clause doesn’t state, or the obligation type is set to Hold for review in Obligation Types. The reason is in the item’s Notes. Accept, edit or exclude it on the Review page. Held items aren’t assigned or reminded until someone confirms them.

What do High, Medium and Low risk mean?
High
Missing it automatically loses a right or money or starts a legal clock (notice, renewal and option deadlines we must act on; change-of-control and breach notices; financing covenants, compliance certificates and default notices).
Medium
Other dated or money obligations (payments, reports, certificates, audits).
Low
Ongoing conduct, and counterparty duties we only monitor.

High-risk items are counted on their own on the Leadership page and in the Friday digest. If an item’s risk doesn’t fit, change it in the Obligations list.

How do I remove an obligation?

Select it in the Obligations list and use the Remove obligation quick step. It takes the item out of tracking and records the clause in Exclusions, so the next capture doesn’t add it back. Deleting the item instead doesn’t stop capture from finding the clause again. If it’s simply done, use the Mark fulfilled quick step instead — that keeps the record.

What if a contract is amended?

Upload the amendment to Contracts and set Amends to the original contract’s file name. Adding it starts Capture new contracts, which reads the two together: changed terms update the existing obligations (a 90-day notice period that becomes 120 days, for example) and replaced clauses are treated as superseded rather than tracked twice. If you edit or replace a contract file instead, Capture changed contracts updates its obligations. To run capture again without changing anything, select the contract in Contracts and use the Re-capture obligations quick step. Contract 360 shows the contract and its amendments in one place.

Who sends the reminders?

The person who set up the workflows. AI workflows run as the account that created them, so reminders, escalations and digests come from that account’s mailbox and Teams. We recommend setting them up with a shared obligations account — for example obligations@ your domain — so messages don’t stop when one person changes role or leaves.

How is Timing worked out?

These pages work it out in your browser from today’s date: Closed if the status is Fulfilled, Waived or Not applicable; No date if there’s no due date; Overdue if the due date has passed; Due soon within 14 days; otherwise Upcoming. The list’s own Timing column is refreshed by the daily sweep, so it can be up to a day behind.

Why does a page say “Sample snapshot”?

Each page carries a small set of fictional sample data so it never appears empty. When the site provides live list data, the badge changes to Live and the sample is no longer shown — even if a list is empty.
